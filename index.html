90% of Logitech’s Footprint comes from our products. We analyse this impact by life cycle stage.

There are four key phases to a life cycle analysis (LCA): sourcing and manufacture, distribution, consumer use, and end-of-life. Each of these phases comes with its own set of critical focus areas that will shape the final carbon impact of a product. Our multi-step, third-party reviewed methodology conforms to ISO 14067 and ISO 14044.

End of Life

Impact of disposing of our products

Explainer

We determine end-of-life carbon impact using product and packaging weights alongside regional disposal data for incineration, landfill, and recycling. We prioritize designing for safer, faster, and more effective recycling to maximize recovery.

Progress

Step 1 - Battery Access

Recyclers prioritize battery removal. Designing for quick and safe battery access increases product recycling value. We focus on this design aspect first. Try the battery access game linked above to examples of progress in Logitech products.

Step 2 - Design for Grind

Recycling must be high-speed to be profitable, so products are shredded or hammered rather than disassembled with tools. Effective recycling requires separating all materials, but permanent bonding of incompatible materials can prevent this.

Step 3 - Use materials with next life value

Even materials that are technically feasible to recycle are not widely (globally) recycled due to limitations in value and adoption. For example, all metals (except magnesium) are widely recycled but only specific plastics grades are widely recycled like PP, PE, PS, HIPS and ABS. All other materials aren’t currently widely recycled.
